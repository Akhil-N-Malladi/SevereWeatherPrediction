Model Interpretability

To understand the inner workings of our XGBoost model, we utilized SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values to analyze feature importance and the impact of each feature on the model's predictions. SHAP values provide a unified measure of feature importance by quantifying the contribution of each feature to the prediction for a specific instance. By visualizing SHAP values, we can identify which features have the most significant influence on the model's output and gain insights into how different weather variables interact to produce accurate predictions.

Extreme Heat

Heat predictions are most sensitive to forecast maximum temperature, overnight low temperature, recent humidity, cloud cover, and urban heat retention indicators. The model treats persistent nighttime warmth as a strong warning sign because it limits public recovery time during multi-day events.

Extreme Cold

Cold-risk decisions rely on minimum temperature, wind speed, pressure changes, snow cover, and the rate of temperature decline. Locations with rapid frontal passages receive additional attention because infrastructure and shelter demand can shift quickly.

Extreme Wind

Wind predictions emphasize pressure gradients, convective available potential energy, storm motion, and recent gust observations. SHAP review shows that the model usually elevates risk when atmospheric instability and fast steering winds appear together.

Hail

Hail risk is driven by instability, freezing-level height, vertical wind shear, and storm-relative humidity. The model raises confidence when those ingredients line up over counties with a history of severe convective reports.

Snowstorms

Snowstorm estimates depend on surface temperature, moisture depth, lift, wind exposure, and recent precipitation type. The model is designed to distinguish ordinary winter precipitation from events likely to disrupt travel, power restoration, and local services.

Heavy Rain

Heavy-rain predictions focus on precipitable water, storm training potential, soil saturation, terrain, and short-term rainfall intensity. The strongest signals occur when atmospheric moisture and slow storm movement combine over already wet areas.
